# **2\. Introduction**

In Vietnam, some students have limited opportunities to conduct hands-on physics experiments because laboratory equipment, class time, or supporting resources may be insufficient. As a result, students can find it difficult to connect abstract formulas and theoretical explanations to observable physical phenomena. Our team proposes PhysiX, an interactive web-based virtual physics laboratory intended to complement classroom teaching and real-world practice.

Rather than showing only pre-recorded animations, the platform aims to let students interact with a virtual laboratory: select apparatus, examine and operate controls, adjust experimental conditions, collect virtual measurements, and visualize the outcomes in real time. An AI assistant will support laboratory preparation and step-by-step learning. The long-term vision includes turning a natural-language experiment description into a structured, executable simulation scenario.

The primary educational objective is to make experimental learning more accessible, repeatable, and intuitive. The need for these capabilities and their priority will be validated through interviews with potential users.

# **3\. Target Users and Environments**

* Primary users: High-school physics students (especially Grades 10–12) who want to practice selected experiments outside a physical laboratory or revisit difficult concepts.  
* Secondary users: Physics teachers who want to demonstrate apparatus and experimental procedures or assign virtual practice.  
* Learning contexts: Individual study at home, teacher-supported classroom demonstrations, and supplementary practical exercises.  
* Target Environments 

\- Platform: Web application. 

\- Devices: Primarily desktops and laptops, with tablet support considered. 

\- Operating Systems: Windows, macOS, and Linux through modern web browsers. 

\- Usage: Independent learning at home, classroom demonstrations, and supplementary laboratory practice.

## **4\. Key Features**

1. **Interactive 3D Laboratory:** Explore and interact with virtual laboratory equipment in a three-dimensional environment.  
2. **Physics Experiments:** Conduct selected physics experiments, adjust parameters, and observe simulated physical phenomena in real time.  
3. **Virtual Instruments:** Assemble and operate supported laboratory instruments, perform measurements, and read experimental results.  
4. **Data Visualization:** Record measurements, visualize experimental data, and compare results across multiple trials.  
5. **AI Laboratory Assistant:** Provide contextual guidance on equipment selection, experimental procedures, instrument operation, and troubleshooting.  
6. **AI-Assisted Equipment Preparation:** Suggest and prepare supported laboratory equipment based on user requests, subject to user confirmation.  
7. **Independent Practice:** Allow students to perform experiments independently, with optional AI assistance when needed

### **5.1. Additional Survey**

An online survey was conducted to gather further insights, receiving 25 responses from 10 Grade 12 students and 15 university students or former high-school physics learners.

Key findings:

* **84%** reported having performed no more than two hands-on physics experiments during their most recent year of physics education.  
* **64%** selected the ability to modify experimental parameters and repeat experiments as a preferred feature.  
* **60%** selected interactive 3D equipment manipulation.  
* **72%** preferred AI assistance that responds to user requests, provides optional guidance, or prepares equipment with user confirmation.  
* **84%** expressed willingness or potential interest in trying PhysiX, including those who wanted to see a working demonstration first.

These preliminary findings support the demand for interactive experimentation and user-controlled AI assistance. However, the survey represents a limited, self-selected sample.

Evidence: [PhysiX - Ket qua khao sat](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1NtrLPQ92y4cZhb7aUydLoApX7UXdE6QLP9Fi7cq1uxA/edit?usp=sharing)

Based on these findings, PhysiX will prioritize interactive experimentation, virtual measurement instruments, and optional AI guidance, while emphasizing physical accuracy and independent learning.
